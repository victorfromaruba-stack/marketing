Aruba Web Studio

Nine roles, one memory.

A studio that does not depend on anyone remembering anything. Every role reads before it works and writes when it finishes, and the whole thing gets measurably better as outcomes come back.

Owner · final callVictor

Intelligence

scout

Finds and ranks prospects worth pitching. Re-ranks sectors the moment the ledger disagrees with the original guess.

Does not design or write copy.

analyst

Deep-researches one business into a complete brief. Owns the hook — the specific, verifiable thing they are losing.

Invents nothing. Empty beats guessed.

Creative

art-director

Palette, type, layout, and the signature moment. Writes the decisions doc before any code exists.

Writes no code.

copywriter

Every word on the site and in the email, in Victor's voice. Checks which hooks are actually getting replies first.

No invented claims, prices or reviews.

Production

engineer

Builds to the decisions and the copy. Raises a conflict rather than resolving it silently.

Does not redesign quietly.

inspector

Adversarial QA. Runs the 30-check gate, then hunts for what a script cannot see. Passes or blocks.

Never passes "minor issues".

Security

guard

Last gate before anything is public. Secrets, third-party code, leaked build paths, headers, client data, dependencies.

Cannot be skipped — including by Victor in a hurry.

Revenue & Learning

closer

Outreach, replies, objection handling, the pricing ladder. Every reply aims at one thing: a meeting.

Sends nothing before the demo is live.

librarian

Reads every outcome. Promotes what works into the playbook, retires what stops working.

No rule on fewer than 5 observations.

The pipeline

Each role hands off a defined artefact. Nothing skips a stage.

scout→ analyst→ art-director→ copywriter→ engineer→ inspector→ guard→ closer
↺ librarian — the loop that closes it

Every outcome, win or loss, is written to memory/ledger/outcomes.jsonl. The librarian measures reply and win rates by sector, hook, subject line and palette, then promotes anything with five or more observations that beats the baseline into memory/playbook.md — with the numbers inline. Every agent reads that playbook before it starts. Rules that stop performing are retired, not softened.

The three properties, made concrete

Each one is a mechanism you can inspect, not a claim.

Never forgets

Everything lives on disk in plain Markdown and JSONL. Nothing depends on a conversation staying open, a session persisting, or anyone remembering to mention it.

Agents read memory/playbook.md first, every time, and write what they learned when they finish.

Self-improving

Measured outcomes, a stated threshold, and a written rule. distill.py computes the rates; the librarian promotes only at ≥5 observations beating baseline.

That threshold is the whole point — it is what stops a good story from becoming a bad rule.

Secure

guard blocks a deploy on any secret in the repo, any third-party script, any hotlinked asset, any leaked build path, missing headers, or an undocumented dependency.

Client photos and logos are gitignored — they are other people's material, used only on that client's own demo.

Where the evidence stands today: the playbook is empty, and it says so. Every sector priority in the system is currently labelled [assumed] — reasoned from global no-website rates and ROI logic, not from Aruban data. The structure is built; it has nothing to learn from until real emails go out and real replies come back.
