I run my day with AI agents.

Most of what I teach, I learned by building it for myself and fixing it when it broke.

I’m Victor. I work full time in hospitality, and I build websites and apps for Aruba businesses through Aruba Webstudio. Around both, I set up a team of AI agents that run on my own server. They handle the boring, repeatable parts of my week, and they tell me when something needs a person.

You don’t need a computer science degree for any of this. You need patience and a few good habits, and I can save you most of the mistakes I made.

Every morning
An agent opens a few of the websites I’ve built, the way a visitor would, and writes up anything that’s wrong.
All day
Agents read incoming email and messages, pull out what matters, and fill in spreadsheets I used to update by hand.
When it matters
A serious problem sends an alert to my phone, so I don’t have to keep checking.
Every job
Each agent writes down what it did, so I can read yesterday’s work and catch it when it’s wrong.

They get things wrong too. A good part of what I teach is how to notice, and the safety rules I follow because I learned them the hard way.

The rules come before the tools.

An assistant that can act for you can also act against you. These come first in every level.

  1. 1
    Keys never go in a chatPasswords and API keys live on your machine. You won’t paste them into a message, a screenshot or a shared document.
  2. 2
    No spending powerYour agent never gets a card, a bank login or a way to buy things, and every account it uses has a monthly limit.
  3. 3
    Other people’s data stays outClients, guests, patients and staff don’t go into an AI tool without their agreement. In class we only use made-up data.
  4. 5
    It asks before it sendsNo messages, posts or deletions without your yes, at least until you’ve watched it work for weeks.
  5. 7
    You can always switch it offYou know the one command that stops it, and you can read what it did yesterday.
  6. 10
    It isn’t open to the internetIts dashboard and controls stay private to you. We set up the firewall together and check it from outside.

Questions people ask

Do I need to know how to code?

Not for Level 1. Level 2 means typing a few commands, and I’ll walk you through each one. Level 3 is where you build things, with Claude Code doing most of the writing.

Which language are the classes in?

The tools and handouts are in English. In the room we can talk in Papiamento, Spanish or English. The AI handles English and Spanish well, and its Papiamento needs checking, which we practise.

Mac or Windows?

Level 1 works on anything, including a phone. Level 2 needs a Mac, a Windows 11 laptop or a Linux machine you’re allowed to install software on. A locked work laptop won’t do. If yours can’t run it, you can rent a small server in your own name for the length of the course.

How do I pay?

Level 1 is ƒ175, paid in cash at session 1. If session 1 isn’t for you, tell me before session 2 and you get your money back.

Is there anything to pay on top?

Not for Level 1: we use free accounts. For Levels 2 and 3 you need your own Claude API account, paid by you, with a hard monthly limit that you choose before anything runs. If you’d rather use a small rented server than your laptop, you rent it in your own name for a few dollars a month.
