## Read this first: the one thing that will make or break this

You said you'll be emailing everyone. Here's the problem with "everyone" in Aruba, and it's a maths problem, not an opinion:

**There are only about 1,500–2,500 businesses on this island worth pitching. That's your entire universe.**

The 2026 industry benchmark for cold email is a **3.43% reply rate**. Elite campaigns hit 10.7%. So:

| Approach | Emails sent | Replies | Real conversations | Likely clients |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blast everyone, generic | 2,000 | ~69 | ~25 | **5–8** |
| Personalised, demo-led | 600 | ~65 | ~40 | **12–20** |

Same result from a third of the volume — **and here's the part that matters: you only get one shot at each business.** In a normal market you can burn a list and buy another. There is no other list in Aruba. Once a restaurant owner has deleted your generic email, he has categorised you, and on an island of 108,000 he tells other owners.

**So: email everyone — but email them one at a time, well, in waves.** 20–30 a day, each with a real first line about their actual business. That's 600–900 a month. You'll get through your whole addressable market in a quarter and you won't have burned it.

The single highest-leverage move: **put a link to a real demo site in the first email.** It converts email into the walk-in demo play from the playbook, at scale. Section 4 covers how to make that cheap.

## 1. Sending setup — do this before you send a single email

If you skip this section your emails go to spam and none of the rest matters.

### Buy a second domain

**Never send cold email from your main domain.** If it gets flagged, your business email dies with it.

- Main site: `yourbusiness.com`
- Cold outreach: `yourbusiness.co` or `getyourbusiness.com` or `yourbusiness-aw.com`
- Cost: ~$12/year. Cheapest insurance you will ever buy.

### Set up authentication (non-negotiable)

Google enforces this with **permanent 550 rejections** as of November 2025 — not soft deferrals like before. Microsoft Outlook began enforcement in May 2025.

| Record | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **SPF** | Says which servers may send as you | DNS TXT record |
| **DKIM** | Cryptographically signs your mail | Generated by Google Workspace |
| **DMARC** | Tells receivers what to do with fails — start at `p=none` | DNS TXT record |
| **Custom tracking domain** | Stops shared-tracking-domain reputation damage | Your sending tool |

Google Workspace (~$7/user/month) generates SPF and DKIM for you in the admin console. Set them, then verify at **mxtoolbox.com** or **dmarcian.com** before sending. If SPF or DKIM doesn't align with your From domain, you're in spam.

### Warm the domain — 14 to 21 days, no shortcuts

Accounts that warm properly hit **94% inbox placement by day 21. Accounts that skip it: 61%.**

| Days | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Warm-up tool only. 10–15 emails/day. Zero cold sends. |
| 4–10 | Warm-up continues + 5–15 cold emails/day |
| 11–21 | Ramp to 25–30 cold emails/day |
| 22+ | Steady state + keep 5–10 warm-up emails running forever |

Use a warm-up tool (Instantly, Mailreach, Warmbox — ~$30–50/month). It sends and replies to real inboxes automatically to build reputation.

### Volume limits — the hard numbers

- **Safe: 50–100 cold emails per mailbox per day.** For Aruba, stay at **20–30** — you don't have the list to justify more, and lower volume means better personalisation.
- **Over 150/day per mailbox: 43% higher spam rate.** At 200+: spam rates over 16%.
- **Stop and diagnose immediately if:** bounce rate over 3%, spam complaints over 0.1%, or inbox placement under 85%.

### Stay clean and legal

You're under the 5,000/day threshold that triggers formal bulk-sender rules, but do these anyway — Gmail's filter doesn't care about thresholds:

- Real name, real phone number, real business address in every signature
- A plain-English opt-out line: *"If you'd rather I didn't email again, just reply 'no' and I'll take you off."* — honour it same day
- No image-heavy HTML, no tracking pixel spam, **no attachments**
- Plain text or very light formatting. It should look like a person wrote it, because a person did.
- **Verify addresses before sending.** Bounces kill reputation faster than anything else. Use NeverBounce/ZeroBounce (~$0.008/email).

**One extra reason to keep this clean that has nothing to do with Gmail:** Aruba is 108,000 people. A campaign that reads as spam will be forwarded, screenshotted and discussed in Facebook groups. Your name is the asset. Protect it.

## 2. Finding the email addresses

This is the actual work. Budget a full week.

**Sources, in order of yield:**

1. **Google Maps** — search each category × each district (Oranjestad, Noord, Palm Beach, Eagle Beach, Malmok, San Nicolas, Santa Cruz, Savaneta, Paradera, Piedra Plat). Many listings show a website or email. Log the ones with *no* website — those are your prospects.
2. **Facebook business pages** — most Aruban businesses have one, and most list an email in the About tab. This will be your single biggest source.
3. **FindYello Aruba** and the **VisitAruba business directory** — structured listings, easy to work through.
4. **AHATA member list** — 151 tourism companies, most already have sites, but their *suppliers* don't.
5. **Instagram bios** — tour operators and food businesses often put email there.
6. **TripAdvisor / Viator listings** — tour operators paying commission. Excellent targets. Names visible, emails often findable from there.

**Reality check:** expect maybe **50–65% of Aruban small businesses to have a findable email.** Many operate purely on WhatsApp and Messenger. That's not a failure of your list — it's a signal. Section 7 covers what to do with the rest.

**Rule: no generic `info@` blast.** Find the owner's name. "Hi Ricardo" beats "Dear Business Owner" by a mile, and on this island the owner *is* the business. If you can only find `info@`, still open with the owner's name if you know it from the Facebook page.

## 3. The sequence

**4–7 touches, 3–4 days apart.** Fewer than four leaves money on the table; more than seven has diminishing returns unless each one adds something new.

**The first email gets 58% of all replies.** Put your best material there. The follow-ups matter, but nothing rescues a weak opener.

| Touch | Day | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 0 | The demo link. Your whole shot. |
| **2** | 4 | Short bump — "did it come through?" |
| **3** | 8 | New value — a specific search result, a competitor, a number |
| **4** | 14 | The ownership argument |
| **5** | 21 | The break-up |

**Never send a follow-up that just says "just checking in."** Every touch adds one new fact.

Send Tuesday–Thursday. **Wednesday shows peak engagement; Monday is best for launching a new sequence.** Send between 08:00–10:00 Aruba time — small business owners read email before the day starts.

## 4. Making the demo cheap enough to do at scale

The demo link is what makes this work. But you can't build 600 sites.

**The tiered approach:**

- **Tier A — top 50 prospects (tours, guesthouses, best restaurants):** build a real one-page demo. 60–90 minutes each once your template is done. Pull their photos and content from their Facebook page.
- **Tier B — next 200:** build **one** demo per sector, generic but polished, with *their* business name and logo dropped in. 10 minutes each with a template and a find-replace. Link says "here's what yours would look like."
- **Tier C — everyone else:** no demo. Use the search-result screenshot instead (free, 2 minutes, still concrete).

Host demos free on Cloudflare Pages: `demo.yourdomain.com/business-name`. Set them to auto-expire from your list after 30 days so you're not maintaining 600 dead pages.

**The screenshot alternative, for when you can't build:** search their category on Google, screenshot the results with their competitor visible and them absent, and put it in the email as a link (not an attachment). Costs you two minutes and lands almost as hard.

## 5. The emails

Keep every one **under 80 words**. Campaigns under 80 words outperform longer ones — brevity forces clarity, and a busy shop owner on a phone will not scroll.

Format rules: no logo, no header image, no HTML template. Plain text. One link maximum. Signature is name, phone, one line.

## 7. What to do about the ones with no email

Half your market has no findable email address. Don't lose them.

| Channel | How | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Facebook Messenger** | Message the business page directly | Highest yield for Aruba. Same scripts, shorter. |
| **Instagram DM** | For food, salons, tours | Owner usually runs it personally |
| **WhatsApp Business** | Number listed on their page | Most personal — but only if the number is publicly published as a business contact |
| **Walk in** | Section 8 of the playbook | Still the highest close rate. Reserve for your top 30. |

**Messenger version of the pitch (shorter still):**

> Hi [Name], I searched "[category] Aruba" today and [Business] didn't come up, but three of your competitors did. I built you a page to fix that: [link]. It's live, free to look at, and I'll delete it Friday if you don't want it. Victor

**Realistic split for Aruba:** roughly 50% email, 35% Messenger/Instagram, 15% walk-in for the highest-value targets. Track all three in the same spreadsheet.

## 8. Handling the replies

The replies are where the money is. Respond within **two hours** during business hours — speed of reply is one of the strongest predictors of closing.

| They reply | You reply |
|---|---|
| **"How much?"** | "$650 to build it, $35/month after that for hosting, updates and support. Or $995 for the build plus the first full year, which is the one most people take. Want to see the three options?" *(Never answer with just the number. Always attach the ladder.)* |
| **"Looks good, but I need to think."** | "Of course. Can I keep the page live another two weeks so you're deciding on something real? No cost, and I won't chase you." |
| **"I already have a website."** | "I saw it. [One specific honest observation: it's slow on mobile / the last post is from 2021 / the contact form is broken.] Want me to check it properly and send you a short list of what's costing you? Free, takes me 20 minutes." *(This converts into rebuild work regularly.)* |
| **"Facebook works fine for me."** | "For locals it genuinely does. You're not wrong. This is about the 1.5 million visitors who search Google before they land and never see local Facebook pages. Different customer entirely." |
| **"Too expensive."** | "Afl. 63 a month. What's one customer worth to you? If it brings one a month it's already paid. And if it does nothing in 90 days I'll refund the monthly. I only make money if you keep it." |
| **"Who are you?"** | Short, human, local. "I'm Victor, I'm from here, I build websites for local businesses. Here's mine: [link]. Happy to meet in person if that's easier. I'd honestly prefer it." *(Then meet. Always convert email into a face on this island.)* |
| **"Send me more info."** | Do NOT send a brochure. "Easier if I just show you. 15 minutes, I'll come to you. Wednesday or Thursday?" |
| **"Take me off your list."** | "Done, sorry for the bother. Good luck this season." *Remove immediately.* No pitch, no last word. |

**The goal of every reply is one thing: get off email and into a room.** Email is how you get the meeting. It is not how you close a $500 sale to a hardheaded shop owner in Aruba.

## 9. Numbers to expect, and when to worry

| Metric | Bad | OK | Good |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bounce rate | >3% | 1–3% | <1% |
| Reply rate | <2% | 3.4% (benchmark) | 6%+ |
| Positive reply rate | <1% | 1–2% | 3%+ |
| Spam complaints | >0.1% | — | ~0% |
| Meetings per 100 emails | <1 | 2–3 | 5+ |
| Close rate, meeting → client | <20% | 30% | 50%+ *(with a demo built)* |

**A realistic first quarter, sending 25/day, five days a week:**

- ~1,500 emails sent
- ~50–90 replies
- ~25–40 real conversations
- ~15–25 meetings
- **~6–12 clients = $3,000–$6,000 in build fees + $120–240/month recurring**

That is a genuinely good outcome. If you're getting under 2% reply rate after 300 emails, the problem is almost always one of three things, in this order: **(1) your first line isn't specific to their business, (2) you have no demo link, (3) you're in the spam folder.** Check them in that order.

## 10. Your first two weeks

**Week 1 — build the machine, send nothing**

1. Buy the outreach domain, set up Google Workspace
2. Configure SPF, DKIM, DMARC. Verify at mxtoolbox.com
3. Start the warm-up tool running
4. Build your own website — it goes in every signature and every sceptic will check it
5. Build the three sector templates from the playbook
6. Start the prospect spreadsheet — target 200 businesses logged with email, owner name, and website status

**Week 2 — build demos, start small**

7. Build 10 real demos for your best Tier-A prospects
8. Send **5 emails a day**, all Tier A, all with a demo link
9. Watch bounce rate and replies obsessively
10. Reply to everything within two hours. Convert every warm reply into a meeting.

**Week 3+ — ramp**

11. Move to 25–30/day
12. Keep 10 live demos in rotation at all times
13. Review numbers every Friday against the table in Section 9
