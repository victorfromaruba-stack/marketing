## 1. The short version

**The opportunity is real, but it is not the one you think it is.**

Aruba has 97.2% internet penetration and 78.7% of the *entire population* on Facebook — that is not an unconnected island. The gap is not that Arubans aren't online. The gap is that **the businesses are on Facebook and Instagram, and nowhere else.** They are invisible to Google, invisible to a tourist searching from a hotel room in Palm Beach, and they own nothing.

That reframe changes your whole pitch. "You need a website" loses. **"You are renting your business from Meta, and you are invisible to the 1.5 million tourists a year who search Google before they book"** wins.

**The honest warning:** at $500 + $20/month you are the cheapest option on an island of 108,000 people. The math works, but the ceiling is low and it arrives fast. Section 7 shows the numbers. Section 6 shows how to fix it without raising your headline price.

## 3. Why "I don't need a website" is half-right (and why that matters)

You said people are hardheaded. They are — but they are not stupid, and if you walk in treating them as stupid you will lose the room in ten seconds. Here is what they are actually right about:

**They're right that:**
- For reaching *locals*, Facebook + WhatsApp genuinely does most of the job. On a 108k island, word of mouth is a real distribution channel.
- Most websites sold to small businesses *are* useless — a brochure nobody visits, built once and abandoned.
- They've probably been burned. Someone built them a site in 2019, disappeared, the domain expired, and now it shows a parking page. That memory is your biggest competitor.

**They're wrong that:**
- Facebook reaches tourists. It largely doesn't. A US visitor searching from their hotel doesn't see local Facebook pages.
- They own their audience. Meta can throttle reach, suspend a page, or change the algorithm and there is no appeal. **Ask them: "if Facebook deleted your page tomorrow morning, how do customers find you?"** — this question lands harder than any statistic.
- A website is a cost. Structured right, it's a booking form, a menu that doesn't need re-uploading, a price list that stops the same WhatsApp question 40 times a week, and a Google Maps listing that actually converts.

**Strategic conclusion: stop selling "a website."** Sell the specific thing their business is losing. See Section 9.

## 4. Who to target — ranked

Ranked on four factors: (a) how many lack a site, (b) ability to pay $500 without thinking, (c) how fast a site pays for itself, (d) how easy the "proof" is.

### Tier 1 — Attack these first

**1. Small tour / activity / water-sports operators**
Snorkel and dive trips, UTV and jeep tours, sunset sails, kite and windsurf schools, fishing charters, horseback riding.
- Customer is ~100% tourist, ~100% Google-first, booking *before* arrival or from a hotel room
- Single booking is often $75–$200 — **one extra booking a month pays for the whole year of service**
- Easy proof: search "aruba snorkel tour" and show them who's on page 1 (usually Viator, TripAdvisor and 3 competitors — not them)
- They're already paying 20–30% commission to OTAs. **Your pitch: "a direct booking form saves you the commission on every booking it takes."** That is a hard financial argument they cannot wave away.

**2. Guesthouses, apartments, small B&Bs, vacation rentals**
- Same logic, bigger numbers: paying 15–18% to Airbnb/Booking on every stay
- One direct booking of a 5-night stay saves more than your entire annual fee
- Very strong ROI story; often the easiest first "yes"

**3. Restaurants, beach bars and food trucks outside the resort strip**
- Highest-volume category; tourists search "restaurants near me" constantly
- Killer feature: **a real menu page that Google can read.** A menu posted as a Facebook photo is invisible to search and unreadable on a phone.
- Add online reservation + WhatsApp order button and you've changed their operations, not just their marketing

### Tier 2 — Steady, less glamorous, better retention

**4. Trades and contractors** — plumbers, electricians, AC/refrigeration, roofers, pool service, landscapers, solar installers
- Globally 45–56% have no site — the highest no-website rate of any category
- Locals *do* search "aircon repair Aruba" at 11pm when it breaks
- One job is often $300–$2,000. ROI argument is trivial.
- **Best retention in the whole list** — they never touch the site, never complain, pay the $20 forever

**5. Professional services** — accountants, notaries, lawyers, insurance brokers, surveyors, translators, clinics, dentists, physios, vets
- Highest ability to pay, lowest price resistance
- Credibility is the entire product here: 75% of people judge a business by its site, and nobody hires a lawyer with only a Facebook page
- Slower sales cycle, but these are the clients who later buy $1,500 of extras

**6. Auto services** — mechanics, body shops, car and scooter rental, tyre shops
- Car rental in particular is tourist-facing and high-ticket; independents compete with Hertz/Avis and lose on search entirely

### Tier 3 — Take them, don't chase them

Salons and barbers (low ticket, high churn, price-sensitive, but visual and good for portfolio), retail boutiques, mini-markets, gyms, bakeries, event/wedding services (actually good — weddings are tourist-driven and high-ticket, but seasonal), real estate agents (mostly already have sites).

### Deliberately skip

Large resorts and AHATA hotel members (already served, procurement-heavy), franchise/chain outlets (no local decision authority), government-adjacent bodies (tender processes), and anything where the owner isn't the one you're talking to.

## 6. Your offer — keeping $500/$20 but making it work

Your instinct on $500 is fine as an *entry point*. The problem is $20/month: it is too low to fund real support, and if you promise "support" without defining it, one demanding client can eat the profit from ten quiet ones.

### The fix: define the $20 tightly, and build a ladder above it

**BASIC — $500 setup + $20/month** *(your stated offer, tightened)*

Setup includes:
- Up to 5 pages (Home, About, Services/Menu, Gallery, Contact)
- Mobile-first, loads in under 2 seconds
- Click-to-WhatsApp and click-to-call buttons
- Contact / enquiry form to their email + WhatsApp
- Google Maps embed
- Google Business Profile claimed and fully optimised *(often worth more than the site itself — do it for every client)*
- Basic on-page SEO, correct titles, meta, schema markup
- SSL, domain connected, mobile tested

The $20/month covers — **write this down in the contract, verbatim**:
- Hosting, SSL, uptime monitoring, backups
- Security updates
- **One content update per month** (change a price, swap a photo, update hours) — up to 30 minutes
- Email support, response within 2 business days

**Not included** (billed at $45/hour, 1-hour minimum): new pages, redesigns, e-commerce, urgent same-day changes, photography, copywriting, ad management.

That "not included" list is not rudeness. It is the thing that keeps you from working for free, and it's also your upsell menu.

### The ladder

| Tier | Setup | Monthly | For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basic** | $500 | $20 | Trades, small retail, anyone testing you |
| **Business** | $850 | $45 | + booking/reservation form, up to 10 pages, 3 updates/month, monthly traffic report, Google Business posts |
| **Pro** | $1,500 | $95 | + online payments, multi-language (EN/NL/ES/PT), review-generation system, quarterly SEO work |
| **Add-ons** | | | Logo $150 · extra language $200 · e-commerce from $600 · Google/Meta ads management $250/mo |

**Present Basic as the floor, not the default.** Show all three. Most people buy the middle one — that is the entire point of showing three. If you only ever show $500, you will only ever earn $500.

### Two structural moves worth more than any price change

**a) Annual prepay.** Offer $200/year instead of $20/month (2 months free). You collect cash up front, you eliminate monthly collection hassle on an island where recurring card billing is awkward, and annual prepayers churn far less. **Push this hard.**

**b) Bundle the first year.** "$500 + first 12 months of support included" = $740 value framed as one clean number like **$650, first year all-in.** Higher cash up front, and the client is anchored on the site *always* costing something — which is exactly the habit you need them to form before year two.

To build a real income you need *both* a steady stream of new builds *and* the ladder in Section 6. Recurring alone will not get you there on an island of 108,000.

### The support-time trap

At 50 clients paying $20:
- Revenue $1,000/mo
- 20 min/client/month average → 17 hrs → **$60/hour.** Fine.
- 1 hr/client/month → 50 hrs → **$20/hour.** You now have a badly-paid full-time job.

**The single most important operational discipline in this business is enforcing the 30-minutes-per-month limit.** Build a simple request form, batch all updates into one day per week, and quote for anything beyond the limit. Every successful small web shop that dies, dies here.

### Costs, tax and compliance

- **Business registration:** If you were born in Aruba with Dutch nationality and you're 18+, you can register a sole proprietorship (*eenmanszaak*) at the Chamber of Commerce **without needing a business licence** — the licence exemption exists for local entrepreneurs. Chamber registration is still required. Confirm your own status with DEACI (+297 521 2400).
- **Turnover tax:** The combined **BBO + BAVP + BAZV rate is 7%** as of 1 Jan 2023, filed **monthly by the 15th**. On $500 that's $35. **Quote prices including tax or you will eat it.** Sources conflict online (some still show the old 1.5%+2% split) — verify with a local accountant before your first filing.
- **Get an accountant early.** Budget Afl. 150–300/month. Monthly filings and a growing client list will bury you otherwise.

### Getting paid

- **Locals:** bank transfer (Aruba Bank, CMB, RBC) is the norm. **Pay.aw** digital wallet is gaining traction (partners include SETAR and Aruba Bank) and is worth supporting.
- **Recurring $20:** hardest part operationally. This is exactly why annual prepay by bank transfer is the recommendation — one transfer a year, no failed cards.
- **Cards / foreign clients:** Aruba Bank offers an e-commerce/merchant solution; Stripe does not officially support Aruba-based businesses, so check current status before promising card payments. For your clients who need to take online payments, this is a genuine constraint — research it properly before you sell a Pro tier that promises it.
- **Always take 50% deposit up front.** Non-negotiable. It filters out time-wasters and funds the domain.

## 8. Proof beats argument — build the evidence machine

You said you want to prove them wrong. Don't argue. **Show.** Three tools, in order of power:

### Tool 1: The Invisible Test (do this live, on your phone, in their shop)

1. Open Google on your phone in front of them.
2. Search their category the way a tourist would: *"best [thing they sell] Aruba"* or *"[category] near me"* while standing in their neighbourhood.
3. Scroll. Their name won't be there. Their competitor's will.
4. Say nothing. Hand them the phone.

Silence after that does more work than any pitch. Then: *"Ta kiko bo ke pa hende haña bo?"* — "How do you want people to find you?"

### Tool 2: The Island Audit (build this once, use it for a year)

Spend one weekend building a spreadsheet of Aruban businesses and whether they have a working site. Sources: FindYello Aruba, VisitAruba business directory, Google Maps (search each category and each district — Oranjestad, Noord, Palm Beach, Eagle Beach, San Nicolas, Santa Cruz, Savaneta, Paradera, Oranjestad West), AHATA member list, Facebook local business pages.

For each: name, category, district, phone, has website Y/N, site works on mobile Y/N, Google Business Profile claimed Y/N, Facebook page active Y/N, owner name.

This gives you three things at once:
- Your entire prospect pipeline, prioritised
- A real local statistic — *"I checked 340 Aruban businesses. 61% have no website. Of the ones that do, half don't work on a phone."* **A local number you counted yourself destroys any imported US statistic.**
- A press/social angle: publish it. "The State of Aruban Business Online 2026." Free credibility, and it makes inbound leads call you.

### Tool 3: The Free Demo (your highest-converting weapon)

Pick your 10 best Tier-1 prospects. **Build each of them a real one-page site before you ever speak to them.** Their photos (pulled from their Facebook), their menu, their real content, on a temporary URL. 90 minutes each once you have a template.

Then walk in with it on a tablet: *"I built this for you. It's live. If you don't want it I'll delete it today — no charge, no hard feelings."*

Conversion on this approach is dramatically higher than any pitch, because you have removed imagination from the equation. A hardheaded owner can argue with a concept. He cannot argue with his own restaurant looking good on a screen in his hand.

Do this ten times. Even at a 30% close rate that's $1,500 and three case studies in your first month.

### Tool 4: The receipt (the thing that makes referrals)

For every client, from day one: Google Analytics + Google Business Profile insights. At month 3, send a one-page report — *"127 people found you on Google this month. 34 clicked to call you. 12 asked for directions."*

On an island this size, **that report is the referral engine.** Owners show it to each other. Get to twenty of those reports and you stop cold-calling.

## 9. The pitch

### The positioning line

> **"Facebook is rented. Google is where the tourists are. I'll get you on Google and keep you there for $20 a month."**

### The three arguments that actually work in Aruba

**1. The tourist argument** *(Tier 1 & 3 targets)*
"One and a half million visitors came here last year — fourteen for every person living on this island — and three out of four were American, each spending about $1,900. They don't have your WhatsApp. They plan everything on Google before they land. Right now, when they search for what you sell, they find someone else."

**2. The commission argument** *(tours, rentals, guesthouses — this is the strongest one you have)*
"You're paying Viator/Booking/Airbnb 15–25% on every booking. A booking form on your own site costs you nothing. Save one $400 booking and you've paid for the whole year."

**3. The ownership argument** *(everyone, especially the stubborn ones)*
"If Facebook shuts your page tomorrow morning — no warning, no appeal, it happens — how does a customer find you? Your website is the one thing on the internet you actually own."

### Language

Aruba is genuinely quadrilingual — Papiamento, Dutch, English, Spanish. Match the owner:
- **Papiamento** with local owners, always. It signals you're not an outsider agency.
- **Spanish** with Venezuelan and Colombian business owners — a significant share of small retail and food.
- **English** with tourist-facing operators and younger owners.
- **Dutch** occasionally in professional services.

For the *client's site*, the default should be **English first** (tourists) with Papiamento or Spanish as an add-on. Say this explicitly — some owners will assume Papiamento-first and you should explain why English serves their revenue better. Multi-language is a $200 upsell per language, and it's near-free for you to produce.

### Where to find them

- **Door-to-door, by district.** This works in Aruba specifically because it's small and relationship-driven — the-lab.nu notes that "relationships and word of mouth still drive much of the purchasing decisions." Cold email will underperform badly here. **Walking in beats everything.**
- Best times: Tuesday–Thursday, 10:00–11:30 or 14:00–16:00. Never Monday morning, never Friday afternoon, never during service.
- **Facebook groups** — Aruban buy/sell and business groups. Answer questions, don't advertise.
- **Chamber of Commerce events, Rotary, business breakfasts.** Small island, high leverage.
- **Referrals.** Offer $75 cash or 3 free months for any referral that closes. This becomes your main channel by month 6.
- **Suppliers as partners** — the guy who sells POS systems, the accountant, the sign-maker, the photographer. They talk to the same owners you want. Split fees.

## 10. How you actually build them (the production system)

Speed is your margin. Everything below is aimed at getting a site from "yes" to "live" in under six hours.

**Stack recommendation:**
- **Astro** (or 11ty) static site, Tailwind for styling, hosted free on **Cloudflare Pages** or **Netlify**
- Forms via Formspree/Netlify Forms → forwards to email + WhatsApp
- Cloudflare for DNS, SSL, email routing — all free
- **Why not WordPress:** monthly hosting cost, plugin/security maintenance, slower, and a 5-page brochure site gains nothing from a CMS. Your $20/mo margin depends on the site needing zero attention.
- **Exception:** if a client genuinely needs to edit content themselves, use a headless CMS or move them to the Business tier.

**Build 3 templates and reuse them forever:**
1. **Hospitality** — restaurant/bar: hero, menu, gallery, hours, reservations, map
2. **Booking** — tours/rentals/guesthouses: hero, offerings + prices, availability/booking form, reviews, FAQ
3. **Services** — trades/professional: hero, services list, credentials, service area, quote form, testimonials

**The intake form is your most valuable asset.** One form the client fills in that captures everything: business name, tagline, services + prices, hours, address, phone, WhatsApp, email, socials, 10–20 photos, 3 testimonials, logo. **No intake form completed = no build starts.** Chasing content is what turns a 5-hour job into a 25-hour job. This rule alone protects your hourly rate.

**Every single site gets, without exception:**
- Google Business Profile claimed, categorised, hours, photos, and linked to the site *(this frequently drives more traffic than the site itself — and it's the fastest visible win you can show a sceptic)*
- LocalBusiness schema markup
- Click-to-call and click-to-WhatsApp above the fold
- Google Analytics installed on day one *(you cannot prove ROI in month 3 without data from month 1)*
- Sub-2-second load on mobile over 4G

**Turnaround promise: 7 days from completed intake form.** It's achievable with templates and it's a real differentiator against agencies that take six weeks.

## 11. First 90 days

**Days 1–14 — Build the machine**
- Register at the Chamber of Commerce; talk to an accountant about BBO filing
- Build 3 templates

## 12. Risks — read this section twice

**1. The market ceiling is real and it's close.** ~1,500–2,500 realistic prospects total. At 5% capture you have 100 clients and ~$2,000/mo recurring. **Plan for this from day one:** the answer is the price ladder, higher-value services (ads management, SEO retainers, e-commerce), and expansion to **Curaçao (~185k people) and Bonaire (~26k)** — same Papiamento/Dutch cultural market, same pitch, largely remote delivery. The ABC islands together roughly triple your addressable market.

**2. The support trap.** Covered in Section 7. Enforce the 30-minute limit or the business slowly eats you.

**3. Being the cheapest.** $500 wins deals and attracts the clients who value you least. Mitigate by always showing three tiers, never discounting below $500 after your first five, and walking away from anyone who haggles hard. **Your time is the scarce resource, not their money.**

**4. Churn.** Small businesses close, especially food. Assume 15–25% annual churn on the monthly plans. Annual prepay is the strongest defence. Never build your income projection on gross client count — build it on net.

**5. Cash flow.** The $20/mo is slow money. Your first 12 months are funded almost entirely by build fees. Don't quit anything until the recurring covers your fixed costs.

**6. You're selling to people who've been burned.** Many "someone built me a site and vanished" stories exist. Being visible, local, reachable on WhatsApp, and still there in year two *is* the product. This is your genuine advantage over every offshore agency in Section 5 — lean on it constantly.

**7. Reputation is instant and permanent on a 108k island.** One badly handled client will be known by twenty owners within a month. Conversely, five delighted clients will fill your pipeline. **Over-deliver on the first ten, without exception.**
